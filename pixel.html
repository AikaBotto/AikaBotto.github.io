Why it slides

A depth sounder fires a fan of sound at the seafloor. The fan spreads with distance, so the footprint of each beam grows as the water deepens — the same instrument that resolves a car in a harbour resolves a city block on the abyssal plain. Getting finer means going lower, which means a robot instead of a ship, which means slowing down by a factor you can't afford across 361 million square kilometres. So the standard bends where the physics does. In their words: “We aim for the best possible resolution, within practical limits.”

The thing I keep turning over: anything smaller than the cell isn't missing from the map. It's underneath it. A 269 m shipwreck on the abyssal plain doesn't show up as a blur. It shows up as nothing, in a square that is officially finished.

Some numbers, so you can check me

Titanic hull 269 m · Ever Given 400 m · Statue of Liberty, ground to torch, 93 m · football pitch 105 m · Boeing 777-300ER 74 m · blue whale 30 m · a two-storey house 12 m. Depths are where each thing sits in the tier table, not where any particular one of them is.
