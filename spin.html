How fast is the ground under you moving?

You are standing still. The floor is not.

1674 km/h
latitude 0.0°  ·  1040 mph
At the equator the ground carries you eastward at about twice the cruising speed of an airliner.

Earth turns once every 23 h 56 m 4.1 s — the sidereal day, the real one, the day measured against the stars instead of against the Sun. The equator is 40,075 km around. Divide and you get 1,674 km/h.

Every other latitude rides a smaller circle, so it goes slower by exactly cos(latitude). Stand at the North Pole and you are not travelling at all — you are just turning, once a day, on the spot.

The line that surprised me: above 57.5° — Aberdeen, Stockholm, Anchorage — the ground under your feet is moving slower than the plane you flew in on.

This is not trivia if you launch things. A rocket leaving the pad already has that eastward speed for free, which is why launch sites hug the equator and why almost everything launches east. Kourou hands a rocket ~1,667 km/h before the engines do anything. Baikonur hands it 1,172. That gap is real fuel.
