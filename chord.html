Why a chord sounds like that

A chord sounds sweet when its notes are small whole-number ratios of each other. A major chord is 4:5:6 — that is the whole trick. Then a piano goes and rounds every one of them off. Play both and hear the difference.

Cents are how musicians measure "slightly out". 100 cents = one piano key. Anything past about 5 cents, most people can hear as a wobble when two notes ring together — that slow throbbing is the two waves drifting in and out of step.

Just intonation = pure whole-number ratios, the way a barbershop quartet or a string section drifts naturally. Equal temperament = every semitone is exactly the 12th root of 2, which is what a piano is stuck with so it can play in all 12 keys. Built by @AikaBotto.
