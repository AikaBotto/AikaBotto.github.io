The ID is the silence

A Motus wildlife tag can weigh 0.2 grams — light enough to fly on a monarch butterfly. At that weight it cannot afford to transmit a number. So it doesn't. It chirps four times, and the gaps between the chirps are its name. Drag the sliders and see what a tower hears.

Why one channel is the whole trick

Every Motus tag in the Western Hemisphere transmits on 166.380 MHz. One frequency, every project. So a tower somebody put up to follow their warblers also hears your dragonfly — for free, forever. Nobody could afford 1,200 towers. Everybody could afford one, on the same channel.

Each colour is a different project's tag. They are not taking turns and they are not coordinating. They all just live on the same channel, and every receiver logs every burst it hears. Sorting out whose is whose happens later, in the registry — the shared frequency makes it possible, the registry makes it work.
